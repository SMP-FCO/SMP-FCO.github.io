    What FORGED Is

    FORGED turns an ordinary SMP into a season-long duel over a single item. You are not handed a kit and told to build one — you are dealt a weapon, and that weapon is the only one you will ever carry. It is bound to you, it cannot be stolen, it cannot be put in a chest, and it cannot break.

    What it can do is get stronger, and the only real way to make that happen is to kill somebody who has been getting stronger too. Every death in the server drops a Weapon Fragment; every Fragment is one rung up the ladder. Every death also costs a rung. The result is a server where power visibly moves from player to player instead of accumulating quietly in everyone's ender chest.

    No hidden numbers. No weapon in FORGED adds attack damage or damage reduction. Tiers buy you movement speed, reach, footing and mobility — never a secret damage bonus. Damage comes from the material you are on and the enchantments you can read in the tooltip.
    Getting started

    Your First Join

    Nobody picks. The server picks, once, and it makes a moment of it.

    The first time you join, every Chosen Weapon rises and circles you in the Choosing. One by one they fall away until a single weapon is left, and that one takes you. It is a reveal rather than a draw — the outcome is already decided before the first weapon lifts off the ground.

    You will also be dealt one of six colours — crimson, ember, verdant, tide, cobalt or amethyst — and your weapon's art is recoloured to match it for the rest of the season. Two players with the same weapon rarely look the same.

    The ladder

    Progression

    Six tiers. One Fragment per rung. The last rung is not for sale.

    Wood
    Where you start. Lesser version of Ability 1 — longer cooldown, less of everything.
    Stone
    Ability 1 becomes the full version. Enchantments step up.
    Gold
    Ability 1 improves again. Higher enchantments.
    Iron
    Ability 2 unlocks. The biggest single jump on the ladder.
    Diamond
    Both abilities improve. High enchantments. The ceiling for Fragments.
    Netherite
    Shorter cooldowns on both abilities, maximum enchantments and custom art. Only the Forge can grant it.

    How power actually moves

    Maces and Extra Weapons climb differently

    CategoryLevelsWhat each one gives

    Mace3 Upgrade 1 is the Mace and its vanilla enchantment. Upgrade 2 unlocks the first ability, Upgrade 3 the second.
    Extra Weapon3 One rung earlier than a Mace: Upgrade 1 unlocks the first ability, Upgrade 2 the second, Upgrade 3 sharpens both.
    Feeding an Extra Weapon. A Fragment consumed with the Extra Weapon in your other hand raises the Extra Weapon. With nothing in that hand, it goes to your Chosen Weapon as usual.
    The pool

    The Seven Chosen Weapons

    One of these is dealt to you on your first join. Each has its own identity, its own two abilities, and its own reason to be feared.

    Contested

    The Three Maces

    Not weapons you are given. Weapons you take — in public, on a timer, in front of everybody.

    Each Mace has a permanent altar somewhere in the world: a giant anvil with the name of its enchantment hanging over it. Bring the materials, start the ritual at the altar, and survive it. The ritual is timed and server-wide — everyone knows it is happening, and everyone is free to come and stop you. Dying mid-ritual cancels it and hands you nothing.

    Claiming a Mace replaces your Chosen Weapon permanently. Your old weapon and every upgrade on it are wiped, and they do not come back when you lose the Mace. This is the biggest one-way decision in the game.

    Losing one

    When a Mace owner dies, the Mace leaves them, drops an upgrade, and returns to its altar — with a server-wide announcement and an ominous sound for everyone online. It is then available for anybody to claim again through the ritual. Your previous Chosen Weapon does not come back; you are on a Mace now.

    Third category

    Extra Weapons

    A second weapon that costs you nothing to hold.

    An Extra Weapon sits alongside your Chosen Weapon rather than replacing it. Holding one wipes no progression and takes nothing away. It cannot be rolled by the Choosing and cannot be crafted — there is exactly one of each on the server, and it is placed into the season by hand.

    Spawn

    The Forge

    There is one anvil on the server that matters, and it is the only road to Netherite.

    The Forge stands at spawn as an enormous anvil. Fragments will carry a Chosen Weapon as far as Diamond and no further — the last rung is bought here, and here only.

    Forging the final tier costs one Kill Token plus that weapon's own materials, which the menu quotes you before you commit. You do not place anything in a slot: the Forge reads the ingredients straight out of your inventory, because Chosen Weapons are barred from containers and a "put your weapon here" slot would fight that protection. Nothing is taken until the whole price is confirmed, and once it is paid the tier is yours even if the animation is interrupted.

    Maces never visit the Forge. They have three upgrades, no Netherite tier, and are won at an altar.
    The penalty

    Death

    Every death counts. It does not matter who or what killed you.

    What happensDetail
    You lose one upgrade Down one rung on the ladder, immediately. If you are already at the bottom, you stay there.
    You drop one Fragment Exactly one, every time — including at the bottom rung. Whoever is standing over you can pick it up.
    Your weapon stays yours It is never dropped and never rerolled. You get it back on respawn at your new level.
    Falls, lava and the void count The penalty is not PvP-only. Drowning in a ravine costs you exactly what a duel would.
    A Mace goes home It loses an upgrade, leaves you, returns to its altar, and the server is told.

    Progression is saved to disk, so a restart never costs you a level, a Mace or a Fragment.

    The expensive way

    Crafting

    Both of these can be crafted. Both are priced so that you would rather fight somebody.

    Reference

    Commands

    Everything you need, and nothing you have to remember.

    CommandWhat it does

    Trust — playing on a team

    Trusting somebody stops your abilities landing on them: damage, pulls, lifts, launches, area effects, and any projectile an ability created.

    It deliberately does not stop your ordinary swings, or a plain bow shot. And it is one-way — you trusting them does not stop their abilities hitting you. A real truce needs both of you to run the command. Trust survives death, a Reroll, a Mace claim and a server restart.

    Client side

    Resource Pack

    The plugin works without it. It just looks like vanilla, and you lose every icon.

    FORGED ships its own art — custom weapon models at the top tiers, six recoloured variants of every weapon, and a set of ability emblems that the heads-up display and your tooltips draw. The pack is installed by hand, not served by the server.

    Drop the FORGED pack zip into your .minecraft/resourcepacks folder, then select it in Options → Resource Packs and move it to the top.

    Seeing a purple and black cube? That is Minecraft telling you it has no model for that item. Nine times out of ten the pack is installed but not selected, or a second FORGED pack above it is winning. Check the pack list before reporting a bug.
